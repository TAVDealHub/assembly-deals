01 · Executive Summary

A Wardrobe of Scents, Not a Single Bottle

WAER sells fragrance as a system. Customers buy a refillable case once, usually as a £48 two-scent edit, and then buy £20 10ml refill vials to rotate by mood, occasion or season. The deck positions this against 100ml bottles that customers are expected to finish before moving on, and argues that younger buyers now layer and rotate several scents instead of owning one signature fragrance.

The founders ran a previous fragrance brand. They say that brand was wrong but the learnings were not, and WAER launches with a 25,000+ opted-in audience, France-based production and an IFRA-compliant supply chain already in place. The company set out to raise £500k at pre-seed. Per our June outreach, it received £750k of commitments at a £1.25m pre-money valuation and increased the round to take them. The round is now closed, and shareholders have consented to a further £100k, allocated first come, first served.

Since the round was raised, WAER has launched at waer.co. The site sells 14 scents as £20 10ml refills, ready-made two-scent edits with a case at £48, a three-scent Discovery Edit at £59, and a WAER+ refill membership. It also offers a style quiz and a 7-day swap guarantee.

04 · Unfair Advantage

The Experiment Has Already Been Run

What the first brand taught them

85% of customers said they don't enjoy shopping for fragrance in store, citing too much choice and no personalisation. Customers naturally rotated scents every 4–8 weeks. Early cohorts showed 60% six-month retention, with discovery driving repeat purchase. Hundreds of Meta ad tests showed portability, rotation and affordable luxury were what converted.

What WAER starts with

A 25,000+ list of opted-in fragrance buyers, so the brand launches to an existing audience. Established perfumer relationships, France-based production and an IFRA-compliant supply chain. A thesis that fixes the first brand's wrong customer and wrong positioning while keeping the right category shift.

The learnings, retention figure and audience come from the founders' previous fragrance venture, not from WAER. The deck states that the earlier brand was wrong.

05 · Unit Economics

Built for Repeat Purchase

The model is designed so the case starts the wardrobe and refills grow it. The deck sets out three stages:

£48
01 Start: two-scent edit with refillable case, 85% gross margin (deck range £40–50)
£20
02 Repeat: 10ml refills by mood or season, or via WAER+ membership (deck range £18–22)
£150+
03 Discover and build: target LTV via creator collaborations and limited drops

The 85% gross margin is stated for the starter set. The deck does not give a margin for refills, a customer acquisition cost, contribution margins or a payback period. The £150+ LTV is a target: at £20 a refill, a customer who starts with a £48 edit needs roughly five further refills to reach it. Now that the business is live, the first refill cycles will show how realistic that is and what customers cost to acquire.

09 · Financial Projections

No Forecast in the Deck

The deck contains no revenue forecast or cash-flow model, and it predates the launch, so it has no trading figures. Investors should ask for launch-to-date sales, conversion and refill rates, and the financial model behind the 12–18 month runway.

10 · Investment Assessment

Balanced Assessment

Why We’d Back It

  • Proven operators. A co-founder who sold two businesses to Groupon, and two senior Beauty Pie alumni who were there from near zero to £75m+ in revenue.
  • Deep fragrance expertise. A qualified perfumer and IFRA director leads fragrance, with production and supply chain already in place.
  • Low entry price. £1.25m pre-money for this team, with EIS relief on top.
  • Strong demand for the round. £750k committed against a £500k target.
  • Repeat-purchase model. An 85% gross margin on the starter set, with refills designed to create recurring spend.
  • Not starting from zero. A 25,000+ opted-in audience and tested messaging from the previous brand.
  • Now live. Launched on waer.co with 14 scents, occasion-based edits, a WAER+ refill membership, a style quiz and a 7-day swap guarantee.

What to Watch

  • Very early trading. WAER has only just launched, so there is little sales history yet. Ask for launch-to-date orders, conversion and acquisition cost.
  • Refill behaviour still to show. The model depends on customers returning for £20 refills. The first 4–8 week refill cycles are the key early signal to track.
  • Retention is borrowed for now. The 60% six-month retention comes from the founders' earlier brand. WAER's own cohorts need six months before a comparable figure exists.
  • LTV is a target. £150+ means roughly five refills after a £48 edit. Refill margin and CAC are not in the deck.
  • Competition. Sniph and Scentbird already sell the wardrobe and subscription model in the mass market.
  • No forecast. The deck has no revenue forecast or cash-flow model behind the 12–18 month runway.
11 · Analyst Conclusion

Verdict: Back, Small Ticket, EIS Essential

WAER is an early bet on a strong team and a clear format idea. The founders have scaled Beauty Pie, sold businesses to Groupon and spent years inside the fragrance category. They are relaunching with lessons from a brand that did not work, an existing audience, and a supply chain already running. The product, now live, turns a one-off purchase into a refill habit, and the 85% starter-set margin gives room to fund acquisition.

The risk is that WAER has only just started trading. Its retention figures still come from the previous brand, the £150+ LTV is a target, and the deck has no CAC, no refill margin and no forecast. The first months of sales and refill data will show whether customers behave as the model expects. Wardrobe and subscription models already exist at the mass end of the market, so WAER's premium positioning and curation have to earn their price.

For EIS-eligible angels, the £1.25m pre-money valuation and an oversubscribed round make a small ticket in the £100k extension reasonable. Because allocation is first come, first served, interested investors should move quickly, but should first ask for launch-to-date trading and the runway model.
